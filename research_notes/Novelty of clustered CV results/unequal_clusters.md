# Novelty of Proposition 11: CV variance with unequal cluster sizes and unequal fold sizes

Scope: Proposition 11 of the capstone (proposals/06 section 6g; proposals/08 section "Unequal cluster sizes (Part 11)"). Searched 2026-11-05, covering literature through November 2026. "Opened" means the full text (or the full documentation page) was read; "metadata verified" means the DOI was resolved or Crossref returned the record, but the text was not read. Anything not opened is marked UNVERIFIED. Quotations are kept short; locations are given as section, equation and page (journal page where known, otherwise PDF page of the version opened).

Notation used below: G clusters, m_h rows in cluster h, n = sum of m_h, fold j has n_j rows and its training set N_j = n - n_j rows, omega_h = sum over j != j(h) of n_j/N_j, a_h and beta_h the cluster loss and training-shift totals of Proposition 11.

## Overall: is Proposition 11 known, partly known, or plausibly new?

### Takeaway
Partly known. The ratio-estimator form, the Kish-type design effect with m-tilde = E m^2 / E m, the consistency of the unbalanced cluster-robust variance, and the CR1 leverage bias (closed form 2, first factor) are classical. The weight omega_h has a structural precursor in the appendix of Austern and Zhou, for folds that differ by at most one row, where it is negligible; no source expands it for fold shares that vary at O_p(G^{-1/2}). No source was found that (i) states the Nadeau-Bengio limit with unequal folds, (ii) identifies the extra "normalization" term from own-row fold means, (iii) recommends dividing fold loss totals by n/K for the corrected t, or (iv) applies the row-weighted versus cluster-weighted target distinction to CV. Those four pieces, plus the P2 heuristic and its measurement at G = 21, are plausibly new, with the caveats in the Gaps.

## Key question 1: what do Kish, Cochran, MacKinnon and Webb, Bell and McCaffrey, Carter-Schnepel-Steigerwald and Imbens-Kolesar establish about variance and leverage bias with unequal clusters?

### Takeaway
Together they establish everything in Proposition 11(b) and the leverage part of closed form 2: the unbalanced cluster-robust variance is consistent with a Gaussian t as G grows (Carter et al.; Hansen and Lee), its CR1 form is biased low by an O(1/G) leverage term that vanishes only when sizes are equal, for a mean (Bell and McCaffrey Theorem 1), the bias is concentrated in large clusters ((M - N_g)/M; cluster leverage N_g/N), CR2 removes it under i.i.d. or identity-proportional errors, and the effective number of clusters, not G, governs the variability of the variance estimate. None of them involves cross-validation, training-set weights or the Nadeau-Bengio correction.

- Closed form 2's first factor is not new: it is Bell and McCaffrey's eq. (3) for an intercept with heteroskedastic cluster totals v_h. ## Key question 2: does any CV paper or package address unequal fold sizes in the Nadeau-Bengio or corrected resampled t-test, or normalize fold losses by n/K rather than by each fold's own size?

### Takeaway
No. Every CV variance paper checked assumes one training size and one test size (Nadeau and Bengio; Bouckaert and Frank) or equal blocks with n divisible by K (Bengio and Grandvalet; Bates et al.; Bayle et al. in the main text). Software computes each fold's mean over its own rows and averages folds without weights, with fold-size weighting available only for the point estimate (scikit-learn `iid=True`, removed in 0.24; mlr3 `macro_weighted` and `micro`). No source applies n/K normalization, or any fold-size weighting, to the fold values that enter the Nadeau-Bengio variance.

### Inferences
- "Own-row" fold means are what every implementation computes (Bouckaert and Frank's per-fold accuracy; `cross_val_score`; mlr3 "macro"). The fold value whose simple average equals the pooled estimate is the fold loss total divided by n/K, since sum_j (n_j/n) e_j = (1/K) sum_j T_j/(n/K). scikit-learn's old `iid=True` and mlr3's `macro_weighted` implement this weighting for the point estimate only. The project's recommendation to use residual totals over n/K as the Nadeau-Bengio fold values was not found anywhere and is plausibly new in the CV literature.
- The device is the survey-sampling linearization of a ratio estimator (residuals y - R-hat x over a fixed denominator) applied to random groups. Its likely classical sources (Woodruff 1971, *JASA* 66(334):411-414, DOI 10.1080/01621459.1971.10482279, metadata only; Wolter 2007, Ch. 2 on random groups with nonlinear estimators) were not opened, so the analogy should be stated as an analogy.
- Because scikit-learn's default GroupKFold (`shuffle=False`) gives deterministic, sample-balanced folds, users who repeat partitions for the Nadeau-Bengio test must use `shuffle=True`, which balances group counts and leaves the row counts unequal. That is the regime in which the normalization term arises (inference from the code).

### Gaps
- Not checked: Dietterich (1998), Demšar (2006), Corani and Benavoli's Bayesian correlated t-test (2015), and LeDell et al. (2015, cvAUC fold weights). They are unlikely to treat unequal folds, but this was not verified.
- The journal version of Nadeau and Bengio (2003) was not opened; the setup is taken from the 1999 working paper.

## Key question 3: is omega_h, or the effect of unequal training-set sizes on the first-order expansion of CV error, studied in the stability or influence-function CV literature?

### Takeaway
Only as a technicality. Austern and Zhou's appendix contains the same object, a point's training-set contributions weighted by fold size and indexed by training size, and note that it simplifies only when blocks are equal, but their blocks differ by at most one point, so it is asymptotically negligible. Bayle et al. allow general fold sizes in an appendix theorem but centre the CLT on the fold models' average risk, so no weight like omega_h arises. Bates et al., Wager, Bengio and Grandvalet, and Arlot and Lerasle assume equal (or nearly equal) folds. No source treats fold shares that fluctuate at O_p(G^{-1/2}) or gives the expansion omega_h = 1 - K^2/(K-1)^2 eps_j(h).

### Inferences
- Because beta_1^{l} scales like (1/l) times an influence function, Austern and Zhou's sum over j != b(i) of |B_j| beta_1^{n-|B_j|} is, to first order, sum over j != b(i) of n_j/N_j times the point's influence: a point-level version of omega_h. They show that its deviation from the equal-block value is negligible when blocks differ by at most one point. The project's case differs in kind: with whole clusters of unequal size dealt at random, fold shares deviate by O_p(G^{-1/2}), omega_h - 1 is O_p(G^{-1/2}) per cluster, and the weighted term is O_p(1/G) only after cancellation across folds (derivation step 3). With the real sizes max_h |omega_h - 1| has median 0.17 at K = 5. Recommended framing: "the weight appears implicitly in Austern and Zhou (2020/2025, appendix eqs. 165-169) for blocks differing by one point; we give it explicitly for cluster-sized imbalance, with its expansion and its order."
- Bayle et al.'s Theorem 8 could deliver a clusters-as-units CLT for the pooled estimate with unequal fold row counts (treating each cluster as a point with loss total L_h), but relating that centring to Err_new of the full model is where omega_h enters, and that step is not in their paper.
- Arlot and Lerasle's weights (each training set normalized by its own size) are the resampling-penalty analogue of the n_j/N_j factor; they restrict to regular or near-regular partitions, so omega_h = 1 identically there.

## Key question 4: does the informative-cluster-size literature (row-weighted versus cluster-weighted targets) cover the CV setting?

### Takeaway
No. The informative-cluster-size literature (Hoffman, Sen and Weinberg 2001; Williamson, Datta and Satten 2003; Seaman, Pavlou and Copas 2014; and, for experiments, Bugni et al.) clearly separates the "all members" (row-weighted) estimand from the "typical member of a typical cluster" (cluster-weighted) estimand, and shows that unweighted estimating equations target the former. None of these papers mentions prediction error or cross-validation. Proposition 11(e) (d = 0 for a row-weighted target even under misspecification; d != 0 in general for a cluster-weighted target) combines these known ingredients in a setting where we found no prior statement.

### Inferences
- In this literature's terms, the project's Err_new (pooled loss over new events) is an "all members" estimand and the unweighted mean of per-event CV error rates is a "typical cluster" estimand. Because an unweighted M-estimator such as logistic regression targets the all-members population, its limit theta* minimizes the row-weighted risk A, so d = grad A(theta*) = 0 whenever the evaluation loss is the training loss. That is Proposition 11(e). For the cluster-weighted target theta* is in general not the minimizer, so d != 0 under informative size; this is the CV analogue of the GEE versus cluster-weighted GEE distinction. The ingredients are standard, but the CV statement and its consequence (the Nadeau-Bengio ratio is 1 for log-loss under the row-weighted target with unequal clusters) were not found.
- The project's A2 designs, where event templates carry their own covariates, are informative-cluster-size designs in Seaman et al.'s sense, so the row-weighted and cluster-weighted CV targets genuinely differ there. That is worth a sentence in the write-up, with these citations.

### Gaps
- Hoffman et al. (2001) and the full text of Williamson et al. (2003) were not opened.
- Searched but not found: any paper on cross-validated prediction error, or internal-external validation, that weights clusters by size versus equally and states which target each estimates. Leave-one-cluster-out internal-external CV with random-effects pooling (de Jong et al. 2021, already cited by the project) weights clusters by inverse variance, a third target that was not examined here.
- Delete-a-group jackknife with unequal group sizes (Busing, Meijer and van der Leeden 1999, "Delete-m jackknife for unequal m," *Statistics and Computing* 9(1):3-8; seen only as a search-result record) and survey notes on grouped jackknives with unequal groups were not opened. They are the closest survey analogue of Nadeau-Bengio with unequal folds and should be read before claiming novelty for the normalization result.
