\subsection{How robust are these results?}

Two of our conclusions are firm and two are fragile.

\emph{Firm.} Pre-date information carries a small but real signal: every model's 90\% interval in
Figure~\ref{fig:auc} lies above 0.5. And the in-date ratings add far more than any pre-date model achieves: the gain
of about 0.26 in AUC is more than ten times the standard error of any single AUC.

\emph{Fragile.} First, the random forest's pre-date AUC of 0.5825 clears our pre-registered threshold of 0.58 by only
0.0025, which is smaller than the variation caused by arbitrary choices. Refitting the forest with five different
random seeds gave AUCs from 0.5732 to 0.5870, and dealing the events into folds with ten other seeds moved the
logistic model's AUC with a standard deviation of 0.0127. The best pre-date AUC also fell just short of 0.58 on
complete cases (0.5796) and when the waves with the different preference survey (6--9), or wave 12, were left out
(0.560 to 0.579). Second, the forest's advantage over logistic regression, 0.0401 under our main specification, was
0.0215 on complete cases, below the 0.03 we had predicted.

Finally, the standard errors themselves were checked by simulation before we used them. With 21 events, our
clustered AUC standard errors were 0.84 to 0.95 of the true spread, so the intervals in Figure~\ref{fig:auc} behave
like roughly 83\% rather than 90\% intervals.
