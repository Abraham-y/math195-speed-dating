\subsubsection{Repeated cross-validation}
Repeating 5-fold cross-validation with several random fold assignments and averaging gives a more stable estimate.
Its uncertainty is harder to judge, because the fold errors are not independent: any two training sets share most
of their data. In fact no exact formula for the variance of $k$-fold cross-validation exists
\cite[Theorem 6, p.~1098]{Bengio:2004}. Nadeau and Bengio proposed a simple correction: with $J$ fold errors whose sample
variance is $S^2$, use
\[
\Big(\frac1J+\frac{1}{k-1}\Big)S^2
\]
instead of the naive $S^2/J$; the extra term $1/(k-1)$ allows for the overlap between training sets
\cite[Section 3.1, p.~248]{Nadeau:2003}. We use it with folds made of whole events.

\subsubsection{Simulation studies}
To check whether an error bar is trustworthy, we simulate many data sets from a known truth and count how often its
90\% interval contains the true value; a trustworthy interval does so about 90\% of the time. With 400 simulated
data sets, such a percentage is itself uncertain by about $\pm1.5$ points \cite[Section 5.2, Table 6]{Morris:2019}.
